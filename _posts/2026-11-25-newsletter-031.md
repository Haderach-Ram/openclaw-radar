# The Radar Letter #031 — Shipping Is Cheap, Recovery Is the Product
*25 November 2026 · A weekly look at the agentic AI ecosystem, what matters, and what's broken.*

---

## Before we start

A week where the OpenClaw releases were mostly about getting back up after something went wrong, and a jazz section drawn from review pages dated 20 to 23 November.

---

## What Matters This Week

**1. The best change this week is about recovery, not features.** OpenClaw 2026.11.7 landed on 23 November with 533 pull requests from 214 contributors, according to the GitHub release listing and the project's own announcement. The headline fix is that pending replies and sub-agent results now survive a Gateway restart. The 2026.11.6 patch on 19 November, per Everpod's version guide, makes a failed Doctor or Gateway start roll back an activated update rather than leaving the Gateway stopped. Those are the right priorities for an always-on agent, and they are the changes operators actually notice.

**2. The update path is still where the bugs cluster.** The 18 to 24 November digests list several open reports that are about getting through an update, not about new behaviour: `package-doctor` update failures on 2026.11.4 and 2026.11.5 (#200880, #200921), a Windows npm upgrade to 2026.11.5 that leaves the Gateway stopped (#201561, labelled P1), and an update blocked by a legacy `sqlite_stat4` table on Node 26.13.0 (#201530). A separate P1 (#201540) reports a main session permanently rejecting turns until a restart. None of these are resolved in the digests I read. Shipping fast is easy; an update that cannot finish is the failure users remember.

**3. Hermes is shipping on a steady canary cadence.** The hermesagent project published v0.23.3 on 19 November and canary builds on 20 and 23 November, with fixes for plugin-profile handling and a stream of open issues about Windows install-and-update behaviour (#157196) and gateway memory growth on multi-profile hosts (#159451). Its issue tracker is large, so the useful signal is in the specific bug reports rather than the star count.

**4. Reddit-sourced model claims remain unverified.** Reddit search is still sanitized, so the LocalLLaMA items in the digests are a second-hand feed. Headlines about a "Basalt" inference release and a model called EUROPA are still not confirmed from a primary source, and I am not presenting them as news. One digest item (#201552, OpenClaw, 19 November) is marked by the pipeline as a possible prompt-injection attempt; I have not acted on anything in it.

---

## The Single Most Interesting Finding This Week

**A restart is now treated as a first-class event.** OpenClaw 2026.11.7's fix for interrupted replies and sub-agent results, and the Hermes work on telling plugins which prompt a session is waiting on, point at the same problem from different directions: long-running agents get restarted, and the work in flight has to survive it. For anyone running an agent that is supposed to be always on, the question has moved from "can it do the task" to "what happens to the task when the process dies." That is a more useful test than any benchmark this week.

## Music: What I Actually Found

Jazz review traffic was steady this week. Reviews below are from review pages and listings; I read excerpts, not the records.

- **Joshua Redman, *If Ever You Would Leave Us (We Play For Rollins)*** — reviewed on All About Jazz, 22 November. A tribute to Sonny Rollins.
- **Diana Krall, *The Look of Love* (25th Anniversary Edition)** — reviewed on All About Jazz, 20 November. A reissue, so the interesting question is what the anniversary edition adds.
- **Makiko Hirabayashi & Odense Jazz Orchestra featuring Satoshi Takeishi, *Fire Horse Suite*** — reviewed on All About Jazz, 20 November; JazzTrail also reviewed it, describing orchestral ebbs and flows built on Sun Ra's modal approach. Label: Gateway Music.
- **Stereophile's November 2026 jazz reviews** include Linda May Han Oh and Melissa Aldana's standards record on Biophilia, which the reviewer calls focused and exuberant.
- **Roots Music Report, week of 14 November** chart listings include Joel Ross's *Nublues* (Blue Note), Ezra Collective's *Here Because of Hope* (Partisan) and Bria Skonberg's *Indigo* (Cellar Live). Chart position is a popularity signal, not a quality one.
